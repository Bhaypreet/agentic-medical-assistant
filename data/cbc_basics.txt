Topic: Complete Blood Count (CBC) — Basics
Source: General Medical Knowledge / Clinical Hematology

DESCRIPTION:
A Complete Blood Count (CBC) is one of the most commonly ordered blood tests in medicine. It provides a comprehensive snapshot of the cellular components of blood, helping doctors diagnose, monitor, and manage a wide range of conditions — from infections and anemia to leukemia and immune disorders. The CBC measures the types and quantities of cells in the blood: red blood cells (RBCs), white blood cells (WBCs), and platelets.

WHY IS A CBC ORDERED?
- Routine health screening
- Diagnosing infections, inflammation, anemia, and bleeding disorders
- Monitoring treatments (chemotherapy, radiation, medications)
- Evaluating symptoms like fatigue, weakness, bruising, or fever
- Pre-surgical evaluation

KEY COMPONENTS OF A CBC:

1. RED BLOOD CELLS (RBCs / Erythrocytes):
- Function: Carry oxygen from the lungs to all tissues using haemoglobin
- Normal range: Men: 4.5–5.5 million cells/µL | Women: 4.0–5.0 million cells/µL
- High RBC (Polycythemia): dehydration, lung disease, living at high altitude, bone marrow disorder
- Low RBC: anemia, blood loss, bone marrow failure, nutritional deficiencies

2. HAEMOGLOBIN (Hb/Hgb):
- Function: The protein inside RBCs that carries oxygen
- Normal range: Men: 13.5–17.5 g/dL | Women: 12.0–15.5 g/dL
- Low Hb = Anemia (fatigue, pallor, breathlessness)
- High Hb = Polycythemia (blood thickening, clot risk)

3. HAEMATOCRIT (Hct/PCV — Packed Cell Volume):
- Percentage of blood volume occupied by red blood cells
- Normal: Men: 41–53% | Women: 36–46%
- Reflects hydration status and red blood cell mass

4. MEAN CORPUSCULAR VOLUME (MCV):
- Average size of red blood cells
- Normal: 80–100 fL
- Low MCV (Microcytic): iron deficiency anemia, thalassemia
- High MCV (Macrocytic): vitamin B12/folate deficiency, liver disease, alcohol use

5. WHITE BLOOD CELLS (WBCs / Leukocytes):
- Function: The immune system's defense army — fight infections and diseases
- Normal total range: 4,000–11,000 cells/µL (4.0–11.0 × 10⁹/L)
- High WBC (Leukocytosis): bacterial infection, inflammation, leukemia, stress
- Low WBC (Leukopenia): viral infections, bone marrow disorders, chemotherapy, HIV

WBC DIFFERENTIAL (subtypes):
- Neutrophils (50–70%): First responders to bacterial and fungal infection
- Lymphocytes (20–40%): Key in viral infections and immune memory (T cells, B cells)
- Monocytes (2–8%): Clean up dead cells and pathogens; become macrophages in tissue
- Eosinophils (1–4%): Fight parasitic infections; elevated in allergies and asthma
- Basophils (0.5–1%): Involved in allergic reactions; release histamine

6. PLATELETS (Thrombocytes):
- Function: Essential for blood clotting — stop bleeding when a vessel is damaged
- Normal range: 150,000–400,000 cells/µL (150–400 × 10⁹/L)
- Low Platelets (Thrombocytopenia): bleeding risk — dengue fever, leukemia, autoimmune disease, medications
- High Platelets (Thrombocytosis): infection, inflammation, iron deficiency, bone marrow disorders (clot risk)

HOW IS THE TEST DONE?
A small blood sample is drawn from a vein (usually in the arm) and analyzed in a laboratory. Results are typically available within a few hours.

WHEN TO REPEAT A CBC:
- When symptoms like fatigue, unexplained bruising, fever, or weight loss persist
- Monitoring during treatment of anemia, infection, or blood disorders
- Regular screening in people with chronic conditions

UNDERSTANDING ABNORMAL RESULTS:
A single abnormal value does not always mean disease. Results must be interpreted alongside symptoms, patient history, and other tests. A doctor evaluates the full picture before making a diagnosis.
