Kidney Function Tests (Creatinine, eGFR, Microalbumin)

Creatinine:
- A waste product filtered by the kidneys; blood levels rise when kidney
  function declines.
- Normal range: approximately 0.6-1.2 mg/dL (varies slightly by lab, sex,
  and muscle mass).
- eGFR (estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate), calculated from creatinine,
  age, and sex, is a more direct measure of kidney function:
  - Above 90: normal
  - 60-89: mildly reduced (often normal for older adults)
  - 30-59: moderately reduced - monitor and address causes
  - Below 30: severely reduced - needs nephrology care
  - Below 15: kidney failure

Microalbumin (urine):
- Detects small amounts of protein leaking into urine - an early marker of
  kidney damage, especially important in diabetes and hypertension.
- Normal: below 30 mg/g creatinine (or below 16.7-20 mg/L depending on lab).
- Elevated microalbumin can be an early warning sign before creatinine rises,
  making it valuable for catching kidney issues early.

Common Causes of Reduced Kidney Function:
- Diabetes (diabetic nephropathy) - the leading cause globally
- High blood pressure (hypertensive nephropathy)
- Chronic dehydration, certain medications (long-term NSAID use)
- Kidney stones, infections, or structural issues

Management:
- Control underlying conditions: tight blood sugar and blood pressure
  control significantly slow kidney damage progression.
- Stay well hydrated, but avoid excessive protein and salt intake if kidney
  function is reduced.
- Avoid unnecessary NSAIDs (ibuprofen, etc.) if kidney function is impaired.
- Regular monitoring (creatinine, eGFR, microalbumin) especially for
  diabetics and hypertensive patients.

Severity Note: Rapidly rising creatinine, very low urine output, or
swelling with shortness of breath can indicate acute kidney injury - this
needs urgent medical evaluation.
