Thyroid Function (TSH, T3, T4)

The thyroid gland produces hormones that regulate metabolism, energy, and body
temperature. TSH (Thyroid Stimulating Hormone) is the primary screening test.

Normal Ranges (adult):
- TSH: 0.4 - 4.0 mIU/L (some labs use 0.4 - 4.5)
- Free T4: 0.8 - 1.8 ng/dL
- Free T3: 2.3 - 4.2 pg/mL

Interpretation:
- High TSH + Low T4 = Hypothyroidism (underactive thyroid). Symptoms: fatigue,
  weight gain, cold intolerance, dry skin, constipation, depression.
- Low TSH + High T4 = Hyperthyroidism (overactive thyroid). Symptoms: weight
  loss, rapid heartbeat, anxiety, heat intolerance, tremors.
- Normal TSH = Thyroid function is currently balanced.

Common Causes:
- Hypothyroidism: Hashimoto's thyroiditis (autoimmune), iodine deficiency,
  certain medications, post-thyroid surgery.
- Hyperthyroidism: Graves' disease (autoimmune), thyroid nodules, thyroiditis.

Management:
- Hypothyroidism is typically treated with daily levothyroxine (synthetic
  thyroid hormone), with periodic TSH monitoring to adjust dose.
- Hyperthyroidism treatment depends on cause - may include anti-thyroid
  medications, radioactive iodine, or surgery in some cases.
- Diet: adequate iodine (iodized salt, seafood) supports thyroid function,
  but excessive iodine can worsen some thyroid conditions - consult a doctor.

Severity Note: Very high or very low TSH with symptoms like severe fatigue,
confusion, extremely slow/fast heart rate, or swelling can indicate a
thyroid emergency (myxedema coma or thyroid storm) - seek immediate care.
