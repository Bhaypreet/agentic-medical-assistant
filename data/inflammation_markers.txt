Inflammation and Blood Cell Markers (WBC, Neutrophils, MPV, Homocysteine)

WBC (White Blood Cell) Count:
- Normal range: approximately 4,000-10,000 /cmm
- High (leukocytosis): can indicate infection, inflammation, stress
  response, or less commonly a blood disorder. Mild elevation is often
  benign and resolves on its own.
- Low (leukopenia): can indicate viral infection, bone marrow issues, or
  certain medications.

Neutrophils:
- The most common type of white blood cell, first responders to bacterial
  infection.
- Elevated neutrophils alongside elevated WBC often points toward a
  bacterial infection or acute inflammatory process.

MPV (Mean Platelet Volume):
- Measures the average size of platelets.
- Normal range: approximately 7.5-10.3 fL
- High MPV suggests larger, more reactive/newly-produced platelets - can be
  associated with increased cardiovascular risk or ongoing inflammation.
- Low MPV can be seen in certain bone marrow conditions.

Homocysteine:
- An amino acid in the blood; elevated levels are linked to increased
  cardiovascular and clotting risk.
- Normal range: approximately 5-15 micromol/L (varies by lab)
- Elevated homocysteine is commonly caused by low Vitamin B12, B6, or
  folate levels - correcting these deficiencies often normalizes it.
- Other causes: kidney disease, hypothyroidism, certain genetic factors.

Management for elevated inflammatory/platelet markers:
- Identify and treat any underlying infection.
- Anti-inflammatory lifestyle: regular exercise, adequate sleep, diet rich
  in omega-3s and antioxidants (berries, leafy greens, fatty fish).
- Repeat testing in 4-6 weeks is often recommended for mildly elevated WBC
  or MPV without clear symptoms, to see if it resolves on its own.

Severity Note: Very high WBC (above 20,000) with fever, or symptoms like
severe fatigue and easy bruising, warrants prompt medical evaluation.
