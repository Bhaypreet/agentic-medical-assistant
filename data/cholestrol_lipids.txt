Cholesterol and Lipid Profile

A lipid panel measures fats in the blood that affect cardiovascular risk.

Components and Normal Ranges (adult, fasting):
- Total Cholesterol: below 200 mg/dL desirable
- LDL ("bad" cholesterol): below 100 mg/dL optimal
- HDL ("good" cholesterol): above 60 mg/dL is protective (below 40 is low)
- Triglycerides: below 150 mg/dL normal

Interpretation:
- High LDL increases risk of plaque buildup in arteries (atherosclerosis),
  raising heart attack and stroke risk.
- Low HDL means less "cleanup" of excess cholesterol from arteries.
- High Triglycerides are linked to poor diet (excess sugar/refined carbs,
  alcohol), diabetes, and metabolic syndrome; very high levels can also
  cause pancreatitis.
- Borderline values (LDL 100-129, Triglycerides 150-199) suggest lifestyle
  changes are needed before medication is typically considered.

Management:
- Diet: reduce saturated/trans fats, increase soluble fiber (oats, beans),
  add omega-3 rich foods (fatty fish, walnuts, flaxseed), limit refined
  sugar and alcohol.
- Exercise: regular aerobic activity raises HDL and lowers triglycerides.
- Weight management: even 5-10% weight loss can meaningfully improve lipid
  numbers.
- Medication: statins are the most common LDL-lowering medication when
  lifestyle changes aren't enough, especially with additional cardiovascular
  risk factors.

Severity Note: Triglycerides above 500 mg/dL carry a risk of acute
pancreatitis and need prompt medical attention.
